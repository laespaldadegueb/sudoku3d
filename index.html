How to play · Sudoku 3D

🎯
Step 1 · The goal
Understand the challenge

Fill all cells of the 3D cube so that no number repeats along any row, column, or pillar in the three axes (X, Y, Z).

Step 2 · Navigate the cube
Rotate and isolate layers

Drag the cube to rotate it and see all faces. On the right you'll find the layer controls: select an axis number (X, Y or Z) to isolate a single layer and reach the hidden cells inside the cube.

The small superscript next to each layer button (e.g. 2³) tells you how many empty cells are hiding in that layer — if you see numbers there, those layers still need filling!

How to play · Kakuro

🔢
Step 1 · The clue cells
Read the black cells

Black cells with a diagonal line contain two clues. The number in the top-right is the sum for the white cells to the right. The number in the bottom-left is the sum for the white cells below.

17 23
23 → sum of cells to the right
17 → sum of cells below
🚫
Step 2 · The rules
Sums and no repeats

Fill each white cell with a digit from 1 to 9. Within each run of white cells, no digit may repeat. When the run is complete, the digits must add up exactly to the clue.

6
1

3
1+2+3 = 6 ✓ · no repeated digits ✓
💡
Step 3 · Tips & controls
Strategy and buttons

Some sums have only one possible combination — start there. For example, a 2-cell run with sum 3 can only be 1+2. Use ↶ ↷ to undo/redo. ⟳ generates a new puzzle.

How to play · Futoshiki

🎯
Step 1 · The goal
Fill the grid like a Latin square

Fill the N×N grid with numbers from 1 to N so that each number appears exactly once in every row and every column. There are no boxes — only rows and columns.

Step 2 · The inequality signs
Respect the order between neighbours

Between some adjacent cells you'll see an inequality sign. The sign always points towards the smaller value. Both the horizontal < > and the vertical ∧ ∨ signs work the same way.

1
<
3
< means left is smaller than right: 1 < 3 ✓
∧ means top is smaller than bottom
∨ means top is larger than bottom

Step 3 · Tips & controls
Strategy and keyboard shortcuts

Start with rows or columns that have many inequality signs — they constrain the possible values the most. Use pencil notes (𝑛 button) to mark candidates. Use ↶ ↷ to undo/redo.

How to play · Takuzu

0·1
Step 1 · The goal
Fill the grid with 0s and 1s

Fill every cell with a 0 or a 1. Each row and each column must contain exactly as many 0s as 1s.

Step 2 · No three in a row
Never three equal values consecutive

You can never have three or more of the same value consecutively — neither horizontally nor vertically. Two in a row is fine, but three is not allowed.

Step 3 · Unique rows and tips
No two rows or columns may be identical

No two rows may be identical, and no two columns may be identical. Strategy: when two consecutive cells share a value, the cells on either side must be the opposite value.
